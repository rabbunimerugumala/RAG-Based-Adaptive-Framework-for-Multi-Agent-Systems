## How photovoltaic cells work

A photovoltaic (PV) cell converts sunlight directly into electricity. Most commercial cells are made of
crystalline silicon. When photons strike the cell, they can free electrons in the semiconductor, and the
built-in electric field of the p-n junction pushes these electrons through an external circuit as direct
current (DC). An inverter converts this DC into alternating current (AC) for household appliances and the grid.

## Efficiency

Typical commercial monocrystalline silicon panels convert roughly 20 to 23 percent of incoming sunlight
into electricity. Efficiency falls as panel temperature rises, which is why panels are mounted with an air
gap behind them for cooling. Dust and shading also reduce output, so panels should be cleaned periodically
and placed where nearby trees or buildings do not cast shadows during the middle of the day.

## Orientation in India

In India, which lies in the northern hemisphere, fixed rooftop panels generally produce the most energy
when they face south. A tilt angle close to the site's latitude is a common rule of thumb for maximising
yearly output.

## Net metering

Net metering lets a household export surplus solar electricity to the grid. A bidirectional meter records
both imported and exported energy, and the customer is billed only for the net consumption.

## Storage

Solar output is zero at night, so batteries are used to store daytime energy. Lithium iron phosphate (LFP)
batteries are popular for home storage because of their long cycle life and good thermal stability.
